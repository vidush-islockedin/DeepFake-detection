    Models used

    Two networks, then two ways of explaining

    A convolutional face detector feeds a Vision Transformer. Neither was built from scratch: MTCNN is used as published, and the transformer started from public deepfake weights that we fine-tuned on a free Kaggle GPU.

    1. 1Find facesMTCNN returns a box and 5 landmarks for every face it is at least 90% sure of.
    2. 2CropA square 1.3 times the face, resized to 224 × 224, keeps the blend edges in view.
    3. 3ClassifyViT-B/16 cuts the crop into 196 patches and outputs P(deepfake).
    4. 4DecideThe face is a deepfake if its score reaches the cut-off.
    5. 5ExplainBlur test per face part, plus a Grad-CAM map of the model's focus.
    Face detection · CNN

    MTCNN

    Multi-task Cascaded Convolutional Networks: three small CNNs in a row, each cleaning up the one before.

    P-Net
    12 × 12 window, proposes faces
    R-Net
    24 × 24, rejects false boxes
    O-Net
    48 × 48, final box + landmarks
    Landmarks
    2 eyes, nose, 2 mouth corners

    Classification · Transformer

    Vision Transformer ViT-B/16

    Every 16 × 16 patch attends to every other, so it can compare the eyes with the jawline in one step, which suits blending artefacts.

    Limits

    What to keep in mind

    • 1The maps show where the evidence was, not which pixels were edited.
    • 2It learned the generators and face swaps in A, B and C. A new kind of generator can still fool it.
    • 3Set C is tested on fewer faces than A and B, so its accuracy is less precise.
    • 4No face found means no verdict. A photo is called a deepfake if any face in it is.
